We present GPU schedules that accelerate graph drawing by stress minimization with stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Our sparse schedule handles over ten million vertices on one GPU.
We adapt the batching idea from mini-batch SGD to group independent updates while preserving the vertex-pair update rule.
RoundRobin Full SGD reuses coordinates across conflict-free updates within tiles. RoundRobin Sparse SGD groups one-sided updates by destination and two-sided updates by disjoint endpoints.
We compare both schedules with sequential CPU and lock-based GPU implementations over 15 iterations from matched initial layouts.
Both schedules shorten iterations on every comparable graph, with median CPU-relative speedups of $7.38\times$ for Full SGD and $44.49\times$ for Sparse SGD. Including preprocessing and setup, the Sparse median is $3.77\times$.
Median final stress increases over CPU are 4.5\% across 19 Full graphs and 0.02\% across 37 Sparse graphs. The largest Full increase is 67.1\%.
With 40 pivots, the sparse schedule lays out road networks of 6.69 million and 11.55 million vertices in 43.45 s and 80.32 s, respectively, including preprocessing.
