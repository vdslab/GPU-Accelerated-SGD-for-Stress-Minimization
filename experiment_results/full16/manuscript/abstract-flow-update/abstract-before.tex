We present GPU schedules that accelerate graph drawing by stress minimization with stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Our sparse schedule handles over ten million vertices on one GPU.
We adapt the batching idea from mini-batch SGD to group independent updates while preserving the vertex-pair update rule.
RoundRobin Full SGD interleaves 16 segments of the inner round sequence across outer rounds, reusing coordinates within each tile segment. RoundRobin Sparse SGD groups one-sided updates by destination and two-sided updates by disjoint endpoints.
We compare both schedules with sequential CPU and lock-based GPU implementations over 15 iterations from matched initial layouts.
Full SGD shortens iterations on 17 of 19 comparable graphs and Sparse SGD on all 37, with median CPU-relative speedups of $9.98\times$ and $44.49\times$, respectively. Including preprocessing and setup, the Sparse median is $3.77\times$.
Median final stress increases over CPU are 0.04\% across 19 Full graphs and 0.02\% across 37 Sparse graphs. The largest graph-median Full increase is 20.8\%.
With 40 pivots, the sparse schedule lays out road networks of 6.69 million and 11.55 million vertices in 43.45 s and 80.32 s, respectively, including preprocessing.
